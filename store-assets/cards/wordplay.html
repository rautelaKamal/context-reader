WordplayShakespeare, Richard III

Now is the winter of our discontent
Made glorious summer by this sun of York;

Plain meaningWhat’s the point?Line by lineHow it works

Richard opens the play by turning a harsh season into a triumphant one through a heavily loaded pun on weather and royalty, establishing his deceptive command over tone and metaphor.

winter of our discontent

The metaphor equates the recent civil wars and the defeat of the House of Lancaster to a bleak, freezing season. By calling it "our discontent," Richard frames the nation's suffering as a shared grievance, masking his own singular ambition behind collective recovery.

Made glorious summer

The verb "Made" places the transformation as an active, sudden event rather than a natural progression. The transition from "winter" to "summer" is abrupt, signalling an artificial, forced shift in the political climate engineered by the new regime.

sun of York

A deliberate double meaning relies on homophones: it refers literally to the heraldic symbol of the House of York (a sun) and colloquially to Edward IV («sun» for son), the new king. It flatters the victor while quietly introducing the wordplay that characterises Richard's duplicitous voice.
