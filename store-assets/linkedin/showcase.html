Shall I compare thee to a summer’s day?

The speaker opens with a question proposing a comparison between the beloved and a perfect day in summer.

Thou art more lovely and more temperate:

They answer their own question: the beloved surpasses summer, being both prettier and calmer — “more temperate”.

Rough winds do shake the darling buds of May,

The first reason summer falls short: violent weather damages fragile new blossoms.

And summer’s lease hath all too short a date;

The second: summer does not last, framed as a rented term that expires far too quickly.
