If the recent acrimony over devolution is any indication, the Centre would do well to disabuse itself of the notion that federal comity is a favour it bestows upon the States rather than an obligation the Constitution imposes.

The central government must stop treating cooperation with states as a voluntary gift it hands down, because the Constitution actually requires it.

Their position

The writer argues that the central government is mistaken to view "federal comity"—harmonious relations between the national government and the states—as "a favour it bestows". Instead, the writer insists it is "an obligation the Constitution imposes", meaning the centre is legally required to treat states fairly rather than acting out of generosity.

Responding to

The surrounding text shows this is a response to recent protests from southern states over the latest Finance Commission award, where they argued they were being "penalised for having controlled their populations", creating "acrimony over devolution" between the central government and the states.
