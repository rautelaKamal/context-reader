VerseShakespeare, Sonnet 18

Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?
Thou art more lovely and more temperate:
Rough winds do shake the darling buds of May,

Plain meaningWhat’s the point?Line by lineHow it works

The speaker opens by asking whether to liken their beloved to a summer's day, immediately deciding the beloved is superior because summer has flaws that the beloved avoids. The tone is reflective and complimentary, driven by the rhetorical question "Shall I compare thee" and the word "temperate".

Shall I compare thee to a summer's day?

The speaker poses a question to start the poem: should I liken you to a day in summer?

Thou art more lovely and more temperate:

The speaker answers their own question, claiming the beloved is both better-looking ("more lovely") and calmer and more balanced ("more temperate") than a summer day.
